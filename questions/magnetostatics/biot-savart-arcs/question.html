$d\mathbf{B} = \dfrac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\dfrac{I\,d\mathbf{l}\times\hat{R}}{R^2}$ with $\hat{R}$ from the element to O. Treat each arc and each straight segment separately: on an arc centred on O, every element is at the same distance and perpendicular to $\hat{R}$; on a segment along a ray from O, every element points along $\hat{R}$. Find the sense in which each arc carries current about O (seen from $+z$): counterclockwise gives $+\hat{z}$ at O, clockwise gives $-\hat{z}$. (b) Nothing. On a segment along a ray from O, $d\mathbf{l}$ is parallel or antiparallel to $\hat{R}$ at every element, so $d\mathbf{l}\times\hat{R} = 0$: each segment contributes zero on its own (it is not a cancellation between the two). The half-infinite-wire value $\mu_0I/(4\pi a)$ applies at a perpendicular distance $a$ from a wire's end, not at a point on the wire's own line.

(d) Biot–Savart is linear and superposable; $\hat{R}$ runs from the source element to the field point, and the order $d\mathbf{l}\times\hat{R}$ matters. An element contributes nothing along its own line and falls off as $1/R^2$ elsewhere, so an arc gives $\mu_0I\theta/(4\pi a)$: proportional to $\theta$, inversely proportional to its radius (smaller for a larger, longer arc). Doubling every length halves $B$. Ampère's law needs symmetry to yield $B$; here a circle about O has $\mathbf{B}\perp d\mathbf{l}$ everywhere, so its circulation is zero for any $B$. The magnetic force $q\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}$ never changes a charge's speed.
