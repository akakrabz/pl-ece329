{{#params.facts}}{{{text}}} {{/params.facts}}
The emf of a moving conductor is $\int(\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B})\cdot d\mathbf{l}$ along it. Here a point at distance $s$ from the pivot moves with $\mathbf{v} = \boldsymbol\omega\times\mathbf{r}$, so the speed, and with it $\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}$, grows linearly along the rod. Work out the direction of $\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}$ with the cross product, not from a picture. In the open rod the charges pile up until the electrostatic field cancels $\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}$ inside, so the end toward which $\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}$ pushes positive charge is the higher-potential end. With a load, the rod acts as a battery; the force on its current opposes the rotation, and the power you supply equals the power dissipated in $R$.
