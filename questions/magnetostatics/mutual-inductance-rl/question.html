Treat it as ideal: uniform field inside, zero field outside, end effects negligible. Its self-inductance is $L_1 = \mu_0n_1^2\pi a_1^2\ell = {{params.L1_mH}}\ \mathrm{mH}$. A short pick-up coil of $N_2 = {{params.N2}}$ turns and radius $a_2 = {{params.a2_cm}}\ \mathrm{cm}$ sits inside the solenoid, coaxial with it and far from its ends. Everything is in free space ($\mu_0 = 4\pi\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm{H/m}$).

{{#params.facts}}{{{text}}} {{/params.facts}}
Follow the chain current → $\mathbf{H}$ → $\mathbf{B}$ → flux → linkage. The solenoid's field is uniform inside, so the flux through one turn of the pick-up coil is that field times the area the turn actually encloses. For the emf use Faraday's law on all $N_2$ turns. KVL: $V_0 = RI_1 + L_1\,dI_1/dt$. The current through an inductance cannot jump, so $I_1$ starts at 0 and rises toward $V_0/R$ with time constant $\tau = L_1/R$. The pick-up emf follows $dI_1/dt$, not $I_1$.
