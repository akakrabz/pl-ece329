(e) Suppose instead that the same expression describes a current density $\mathbf{J} = M\mathbf{r}$ in $\mathrm{A/m^2}$ (so $M$ is now in $\mathrm{A/m^3}$). At what rate is the charge density changing at any point?

For a field that is linear in $x$, $y$, $z$, every partial derivative $\partial F_i/\partial x_j$ is a constant: the coefficient of $x_j$ in $F_i$. The divergence uses the three derivatives of each component along its own direction; the curl uses differences of the cross derivatives, for example $(\nabla\times\mathbf{F})_z = \partial F_y/\partial x - \partial F_x/\partial y$. Curl-free means conservative: zero circulation, path-independent line integrals, a potential with $\mathbf{F} = -\nabla V$, and a possible electrostatic field. Divergence-free means zero net flux out of every closed surface and a possible $\mathbf{B}$. With a uniform curl, Stokes' theorem gives $\oint_C\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = (\text{area})\,(\nabla\times\mathbf{F})\cdot\hat{n}$, with $\hat{n}$ tied to the sense of $C$ by the right-hand rule.

For $\mathbf{F} = M\mathbf{r}$, $\partial F_i/\partial x_j = M_{ij}$ everywhere.

(a) $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F} = \dfrac{\partial F_x}{\partial x} + \dfrac{\partial F_y}{\partial y} + \dfrac{\partial F_z}{\partial z} = M_{11} + M_{22} + M_{33} = ({{params.M11}}) + ({{params.M22}}) + ({{params.M33}}) = {{params.div}}\ \mathrm{V/m^2}$, the trace of $M$.

(b) $(\nabla\times\mathbf{F})_x = \dfrac{\partial F_z}{\partial y} - \dfrac{\partial F_y}{\partial z} = M_{32} - M_{23} = ({{params.M32}}) - ({{params.M23}}) = {{params.curlx}}$, $(\nabla\times\mathbf{F})_y = \dfrac{\partial F_x}{\partial z} - \dfrac{\partial F_z}{\partial x} = M_{13} - M_{31} = ({{params.M13}}) - ({{params.M31}}) = {{params.curly}}$, $(\nabla\times\mathbf{F})_z = \dfrac{\partial F_y}{\partial x} - \dfrac{\partial F_x}{\partial y} = M_{21} - M_{12} = ({{params.M21}}) - ({{params.M12}}) = {{params.curlz}}$, all in $\mathrm{V/m^2}$. The curl of a linear field vanishes exactly when $M$ is symmetric, and its divergence vanishes exactly when the trace is zero. So this $\mathbf{F}$ is {{{params.kind_words}}}.

(c) The curl is the same everywhere, so Stokes' theorem gives $\Gamma = \int_S(\nabla\times\mathbf{F})\cdot d\mathbf{S} = \pi R^2\,(\nabla\times\mathbf{F})_{ {{params.k_ax}} }$, with $d\mathbf{S}$ along $+\hat{ {{params.k_ax}} }$ because $C$ runs counterclockwise seen from $+{{params.k_ax}}$: $\Gamma = \pi({{params.R2}}\ \mathrm{m^2})({{params.ck}}\ \mathrm{V/m^2}) = {{{params.circ_tex}}}$.
