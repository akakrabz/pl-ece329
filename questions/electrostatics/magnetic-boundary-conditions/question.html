Split each field into its component along $\hat n$ and its part in the plane. With $\mathbf{B} = \mu_0\mathbf{H}$ on both sides, $\hat n\cdot(\mathbf{B}_1-\mathbf{B}_2) = 0$ makes the normal component of $\mathbf{H}$ continuous; only the in-plane part of $\mathbf{H}$ can jump at a current sheet. Crossing $\hat n\times(\mathbf{H}_1-\mathbf{H}_2) = \mathbf{J}_s$ with $\hat n$ gives $\mathbf{H}_{1t}-\mathbf{H}_{2t} = \mathbf{J}_s\times\hat n$ (not $\hat n\times\mathbf{J}_s$). In (b), pick either side as medium 1 and point $\hat n$ into it: the other choice flips both $\hat n$ and $\mathbf{H}_1-\mathbf{H}_2$, so $\mathbf{J}_s$ comes out the same.

(a) Normal part: $B_z$ is continuous and $\mathbf{B} = \mu_0\mathbf{H}$ on both sides, so $H_{1z} = H_{2z} = {{params.Hz_common}}\ \mathrm{A/m}$. Tangential part: crossing $\hat z\times(\mathbf{H}_1-\mathbf{H}_2) = \mathbf{J}_s$ with $\hat z$ gives $\mathbf{H}_{1t}-\mathbf{H}_{2t} = \mathbf{J}_s\times\hat z = ({{{params.Js_a_tex}}})\times\hat z = {{{params.jump_a_tex}}}$ A/m. Hence {{{params.a_solve}}}, and altogether $$\mathbf{H}_1 = {{{params.H1_tex}}}\ \mathrm{A/m},\qquad \mathbf{H}_2 = {{{params.H2_tex}}}\ \mathrm{A/m}.$$ Check: $\hat z\times(\mathbf{H}_1-\mathbf{H}_2) = \hat z\times({{{params.jump_a_tex}}}) = {{{params.Js_a_tex}}} = \mathbf{J}_s$. The in-plane component of $\mathbf{H}$ along $\mathbf{J}_s$ is the same on both sides; only the in-plane component perpendicular to $\mathbf{J}_s$ jumps, by $|\mathbf{J}_s|$.

(b) The normal component, $H_{ {{params.u}} } = {{params.Hn_b}}\ \mathrm{A/m}$, is the same on both sides, as normal-$\mathbf{B}$ continuity requires. Call ${{params.u}} \gt 0$ medium 1, so $\hat n = \hat{ {{params.u}} }$ and $\mathbf{H}_1-\mathbf{H}_2 = {{{params.dH_tex}}}\ \mathrm{A/m}$. Then $$\mathbf{J}_s = \hat n\times(\mathbf{H}_1-\mathbf{H}_2) = \hat{ {{params.u}} }\times({{{params.dH_tex}}}) = {{{params.Js_b_tex}}}\ \mathrm{A/m}.$$ (Calling ${{params.u}} \lt 0$ medium 1 instead reverses both $\hat n$ and $\mathbf{H}_1-\mathbf{H}_2$, and gives the same $\mathbf{J}_s$.)

(d) Flux laws applied to a pillbox constrain normal components (the side wall's flux vanishes as the pillbox flattens); circulation laws applied to a thin loop constrain tangential components (the short edges vanish): Gauss's law for $\mathbf{D}$ gives $\hat n\cdot(\mathbf{D}_1-\mathbf{D}_2) = \rho_s$; $\oint\mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{S} = 0$ gives $\hat n\cdot(\mathbf{B}_1-\mathbf{B}_2) = 0$; $\oint\mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = 0$ gives $\hat n\times(\mathbf{E}_1-\mathbf{E}_2) = 0$; Ampère's law gives $\hat n\times(\mathbf{H}_1-\mathbf{H}_2) = \mathbf{J}_s$. The other three are not boundary conditions: normal $\mathbf{E}$ jumps at a charged sheet, tangential $\mathbf{D}$ jumps where $\epsilon$ changes, and normal $\mathbf{H}$ does not jump at a current sheet in free space.
