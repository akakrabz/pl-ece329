Draw a Gaussian sphere with $a \lt r \lt b$, entirely inside the metal. In equilibrium $\mathbf{E} = 0$ on it, so the net charge it encloses is zero. Then use the fact that the shell's own charge is conserved. Continuity $\partial\rho/\partial t + \nabla\cdot\mathbf{J} = 0$, Ohm's law $\mathbf{J} = \sigma\mathbf{E}$ and Gauss's law $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E} = \rho/\epsilon$ combine into $\partial\rho/\partial t = -(\sigma/\epsilon)\,\rho$, so $\rho(t) = \rho_0e^{-t/\tau}$ with $\tau = \epsilon/\sigma$. Solve $e^{-t_f/\tau} = $ the given fraction.

(a) A Gaussian sphere with $a \lt r \lt b$ lies in the metal, where $\mathbf{E} = 0$, so it encloses no net charge: $q + Q_{\text{inner}} = 0$, i.e. $Q_{\text{inner}} = -q = {{params.Q_in}}\ \mathrm{nC}$. The rest of the shell's charge is on its outer surface: $Q_{\text{outer}} = Q_s - Q_{\text{inner}} = Q_s + q = {{params.Q_out}}\ \mathrm{nC}$.

(b) With $q$ at the centre everything is spherically symmetric. A Gaussian sphere of radius $r \gt b$ encloses $q + Q_{\text{inner}} + Q_{\text{outer}} = q + Q_s$, so $$E_r = \frac{q + Q_s}{4\pi\epsilon_0r^2} = \frac{(8.988\times10^{9})({{params.Q_out}}\times10^{-9})}{({{params.r_out}}\times10^{-2})^2} = {{{params.E_out_Vm}}}\ \mathrm{V/m} = {{{params.E_out_str}}}\ \mathrm{kV/m}.$$ {{{params.e_dir}}} Inside the metal ($r = {{params.r_mid}}$ cm) $\mathbf{E} = 0$: the field of $q$ is cancelled there by the inner-surface charge $-q$, which is exactly why that charge is induced.

(c) Only the distribution of the inner-surface charge changes: it crowds toward $q$, keeping the total $-q$, so that $q$ plus the inner layer still produce zero field in the metal and beyond. The outer surface therefore feels nothing from inside and keeps its uniform charge $Q_s + q$; the field outside the shell, and the potential of the shell, are unchanged. The metal screens the cavity from the outside world.

(d) $\tau = \dfrac{\epsilon}{\sigma} = \dfrac{ {{params.er}}\times8.854\times10^{-12} }{ {{{params.sigma_tex}}} } = {{{params.tau_s}}}\ \mathrm{s} = {{{params.tau_str}}}\ {{{params.unit_tex}}}$. From $\rho_0e^{-t_f/\tau} = {{params.f}}\,\rho_0$: $t_f = \tau\ln {{{params.inv_f}}} = {{{params.ln_f}}}\,\tau = {{{params.tf_str}}}\ {{{params.unit_tex}}}$. The charge is not destroyed: it moves to the surface of the block.
