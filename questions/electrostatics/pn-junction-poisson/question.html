In one dimension Gauss's law reads $dE_x/dx = \rho/\epsilon$. Start at the outer edge of either depletion layer, where $E_x = 0$, and integrate across both layers. For $E_x$ to come back to zero in the neutral material on the far side, the two layers must hold equal and opposite charge per unit area. $dV/dx = -E_x$, and $E_x$ is a triangle: zero at both outer edges, peaked at $x = 0$. The potential difference across the region is the area of that triangle, $\tfrac12|E|_{\max}(W_p + W_n)$, and the n side is the higher one.

(a) The field is zero in both neutral regions, so the total charge per unit area of the two layers must cancel: $eN_aW_p = eN_dW_n$. Hence $${{{params.a_solve}}}.$$ The depletion layers are therefore {{{params.p_layer}}} nm (p side) and {{{params.n_layer}}} nm (n side); the more lightly doped side holds the wider layer.

(b) The charge densities are $-eN_a = -{{{params.rho_p}}}\ \mathrm{C/m^3}$ and $+eN_d = {{{params.rho_n}}}\ \mathrm{C/m^3}$. Integrating $dE_x/dx = \rho/\epsilon$ from the outer edge of one layer (where $E_x = 0$), $|E_x|$ grows linearly to its peak at the junction and falls linearly back to zero at the outer edge of the other layer: $$|E|_{\max} = \frac{eN_aW_p}{\epsilon} = \frac{(1.602\times10^{-19})({{{params.Na_m_tex}}})({{{params.Wp_str}}}\times10^{-9})}{11.7\times8.854\times10^{-12}} = {{{params.Emax_Vm}}}\ \mathrm{V/m} = {{{params.Emax_str}}}\ \mathrm{MV/m},$$ the same as $eN_dW_n/\epsilon$ (neutrality). It occurs at the metallurgical junction, $x = 0$.

(c) $V$ follows from $dV/dx = -E_x$; across the region the change is the area under the $|E_x|$ triangle: $$V_{\text{bi}} = \tfrac12\,|E|_{\max}\,(W_p + W_n) = \tfrac12\,({{{params.Emax_Vm}}})({{{params.Wsum_str}}}\times10^{-9}) = {{{params.Vbi_str}}}\ \mathrm{V}.$$ Equivalently, the two parabolas $V'' = -\rho/\epsilon$ matched at $x = 0$ give $V_{\text{bi}} = e\,(N_aW_p^2 + N_dW_n^2)/(2\epsilon)$. The n side is the higher one, as it must be for a field pointing from n to p. Values of roughly $0.6$ to $0.9$ V are typical of silicon junctions at room temperature.
