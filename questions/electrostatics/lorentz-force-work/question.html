(e) Which statement about the selector of part (d) is correct?

The selected velocity is fixed by the two fields alone: particles of any charge, any mass and either sign of charge pass straight through at that velocity. The selected speed also depends on the charge-to-mass ratio $q/m$ of the particles. Positive particles pass along the direction found in (d); negative particles must be injected in the opposite direction. A more highly charged ion (say charge $+2e$ instead of $+e$) must move faster to pass undeflected, because the electric force on it is larger. Heavier ions must move faster than lighter ones to balance the electric force.
$(\mathrm{km/s})(\mathrm{T}) = \mathrm{kV/m}$, $(\mu\mathrm{C})(\mathrm{kV/m}) = \mathrm{mN}$ and $(\mathrm{mN})(\mathrm{km/s}) = \mathrm{W}$. Expand $\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}$ as a determinant and keep the minus sign on the $\hat{y}$ term. $(\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B})\cdot\mathbf{v} = 0$ for any $\mathbf{v}$ and $\mathbf{B}$, so only $q\mathbf{E}$ can deliver power. In the selector, zero force means $\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B} = -\mathbf{E}$: test each candidate direction with $\hat{x}\times\hat{y} = \hat{z}$, $\hat{y}\times\hat{z} = \hat{x}$, $\hat{z}\times\hat{x} = \hat{y}$.

(a) Expanding the determinant (with $(\mathrm{km/s})(\mathrm{T}) = \mathrm{kV/m}$, since $1\ \mathrm{T} = 1\ \mathrm{V\,s/m^2}$): $$\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B} = {{{params.cross_work}}} = \left( {{{params.vxB_tex}}} \right)\ \mathrm{kV/m}.$$ Then $\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B} = \left( {{{params.tot_tex}}} \right)\ \mathrm{kV/m}$, and $$\mathbf{F} = q\left(\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}\right) = ({{params.q}}\ \mu\mathrm{C})\left( {{{params.tot_tex}}} \right)\ \mathrm{kV/m} = \left( {{{params.F_tex}}} \right)\ \mathrm{mN},$$ so $F_x = {{params.Fx}}$, $F_y = {{params.Fy}}$, $F_z = {{params.Fz}}$ mN.

(b) $P = \mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{v} = {{{params.Fv_dot}}} = {{params.P}}\ \mathrm{W}$, with $(\mathrm{mN})(\mathrm{km/s}) = \mathrm{W}$. Only the electric force contributes: the magnetic part gives $(\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B})\cdot\mathbf{v} = {{{params.vxBv_dot}}} = 0$, as it must for any $\mathbf{v}$ and $\mathbf{B}$, so $P = q\,\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{v} = ({{params.q}})\left[{{{params.Ev_dot}}}\right] = ({{params.q}})({{params.Edotv}}) = {{params.P}}\ \mathrm{W}$. {{{params.p_words}}}

(d) Zero net force requires $\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B} = 0$, i.e. $\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B} = -\mathbf{E}$, which points along ${{{params.minus_eE_dir}}}$. For $\mathbf{v}$ along ${{{params.ev_dir}}}$: $({{{params.ev_dir}}})\times({{{params.eB_dir}}}) = {{{params.minus_eE_dir}}}$, the required direction. This is the direction of $\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{B}$. Along ${{{params.negev_dir}}}$ the magnetic force would point along $+\mathbf{E}$ and add to $q\mathbf{E}$; along $\pm\mathbf{E}$ it is perpendicular to $\mathbf{E}$, and along $\pm\mathbf{B}$ it vanishes, so in those directions nothing can cancel $q\mathbf{E}$. Balancing magnitudes, $vB_0 = E_0$: $$v = \frac{E_0}{B_0} = \frac{ {{params.E0}}\times10^{3}\ \mathrm{V/m} }{ {{params.B0}}\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{T} } = {{params.speed}}\ \mathrm{km/s}.$$ Faster particles are bent along the magnetic force, slower ones along the electric force.

(e) The condition $q(\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}) = 0$ is a condition on $\mathbf{v}$ alone: $q$ divides out (so its size and sign do not matter) and the mass never appears, because a particle that feels no force does not accelerate. So the {{{params.particle}}} go straight through, and so would any other charged particle injected along ${{{params.ev_dir}}}$ at {{params.speed}} km/s. Mass matters only after a particle has been deflected, for example in the radius $R = mv/(|q|B)$ of a mass spectrometer.
