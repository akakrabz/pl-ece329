Everything is in the exponent. The real part of the coefficient of ${{params.w}}$ is $\alpha$, the imaginary part is $\beta$, and the wave travels toward the side on which the amplitude shrinks. $\gamma\eta = j\omega\mu$ gives $\eta = j\omega\mu/\gamma$ directly: $\lvert\eta\rvert = \omega\mu/\lvert\gamma\rvert$ and $\tau = 90^\circ - \angle\gamma$. Do not forget the factor in $\mu$. Square $\gamma$: $\gamma^2 = j\omega\mu(\sigma + j\omega\epsilon)$, so $\alpha^2-\beta^2 = -\omega^2\mu\epsilon$ and $2\alpha\beta = \omega\mu\sigma$. Divide the two; $\mu$ and $\omega$ cancel.

(a) The exponent holds $\alpha = {{{params.s_alpha}}}\ \mathrm{Np/m}$ and $\beta = {{{params.s_beta}}} = {{params.s_beta_num}}\ \mathrm{rad/m}$. The amplitude decays, and the phase lags, toward ${{{params.s_udir}}}$: that is the direction of travel.

(b) $\lambda = 2\pi/\beta = {{params.s_lam}}\ \mathrm{m}$, $v_p = \omega/\beta = {{{params.s_vp}}}\ \mathrm{m/s}$ (enter {{params.s_vp8}}), $\delta = 1/\alpha = {{params.s_delta}}\ \mathrm{m}$.

(c) $\lvert\gamma\rvert = \sqrt{\alpha^2+\beta^2} = {{params.s_gmag}}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$ and $\angle\gamma = \tan^{-1}(\beta/\alpha) = {{params.s_gang}}^\circ$. From $\gamma\eta = j\omega\mu$ with $\omega\mu = {{params.s_omu}}\ \Omega/\mathrm{m}$: $\lvert\eta\rvert = \omega\mu/\lvert\gamma\rvert = {{params.s_eta}}\ \Omega$ and $\tau = 90^\circ - \angle\gamma = {{params.s_tau}}^\circ$.

(d) $\tilde{\mathbf{H}} = \hat u\times\tilde{\mathbf{E}}/\eta$ with $\hat u = {{{params.s_udir}}}$: the direction is ${{{params.s_cross}}}$, the size is $H_0 = E_0/\lvert\eta\rvert = {{params.s_H0}}\ \mathrm{mA/m}$, and dividing by $e^{j\tau}$ makes $\mathbf{H}$ lag $\mathbf{E}$ by $\tau$: $\phi_H = {{params.s_phiE}}^\circ - {{params.s_tau}}^\circ = {{params.s_phiH}}^\circ$.

(e) $\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle = \tfrac12\mathrm{Re}\{\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\times\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^*\} = \hat u\,\dfrac{\lvert\tilde E\rvert^2}{2\lvert\eta\rvert}\cos\tau$. On ${{params.w}} = 0$ this is ${{params.s_S0}}\ \mathrm{mW/m^2}$. The plane asked for is ${{params.d_s}}\ \mathrm{m}$ further along the travel, where the power has fallen by $e^{-2\alpha d} = e^{-{{params.s_2ad}}}$: ${{params.s_S1}}\ \mathrm{mW/m^2}$ along ${{{params.s_udir}}}$, so $\langle S_{ {{params.w}} }\rangle = {{params.s_sgn}}{{params.s_S1}}\ \mathrm{mW/m^2}$. The field decays as $e^{-\alpha d}$; the power as $e^{-2\alpha d}$.

(f) $\gamma^2 = j\omega\mu(\sigma+j\omega\epsilon)$ gives $2\alpha\beta = \omega\mu\sigma$ and $\beta^2-\alpha^2 = \omega^2\mu\epsilon$, so $\dfrac{\sigma}{\omega\epsilon} = \dfrac{2\alpha\beta}{\beta^2-\alpha^2} = {{params.s_tanl}}$ (here $\sigma = {{{params.s_sigma}}}\ \mathrm{S/m}$ and $\epsilon_r = {{params.s_epsr}}$). The loss tangent is proportional to $1/\omega$: with the frequency {{params.K_txt}} it becomes ${{params.s_tanl}}{{{params.s_Kop}}} = {{{params.s_tanl2}}}$, and the medium is {{params.s_cls}}. The same material can be a conductor at one frequency and a dielectric at another.
