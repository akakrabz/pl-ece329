A lossless, non-magnetic transmission line has $Z_0 = {{params.Z0}}\ \Omega$ and length $\ell = {{params.ell}}\ \mathrm{m}$. Its generator (at $z = 0$) has an unknown internal resistance $R_g$ and produces one very narrow rectangular pulse at $t = 0$, of open-circuit height ${{params.V0}}\ \mathrm{V}$. The load at $z = \ell$ is an unknown resistor $R_L$. The line is empty before $t = 0$.

A voltage probe {{params.where}}, at $z_p = {{params.zp}}\ \mathrm{m}$, records exactly two pulses and then nothing more: one of height $A$ at $t = {{params.t1}}\ \mu\mathrm{s}$ and one of height ${{{params.rho_tex}}}$ at $t = {{params.t2}}\ \mu\mathrm{s}$.

{{{params.svg}}}

Use $c = 300\ \mathrm{m}/\mu\mathrm{s}$.

(a) Find the wave speed on the line and the relative permittivity of its filling.

(b) Find the load reflection coefficient and the load resistance.

(c) What does the absence of a third pulse tell you?

{{#params.why}}{{{text}}} {{/params.why}}

Find the generator resistance and the height $A$ of the first pulse.

(d) Find the height of the voltage pulse that appears across the load when the pulse arrives there.

(e) The generator's resistance is now changed to $R_g' = {{params.Rg2}}\ \Omega$; the source pulse, the line and the load stay the same. Find the new height of the first pulse at the probe, and the arrival time and height of the third pulse that the probe now records.

The times give the speed. The ratio of the two heights gives the load. The silence afterwards gives the generator. The first pulse has travelled $z_p$. The second has gone to the load and come back to the probe: $2\ell - z_p$. A third would have gone on to the generator and returned: $2\ell + z_p$. At $t = 0$ the generator sees only $Z_0$, so the launched pulse is the source pulse times $Z_0/(R_g+Z_0)$. The open-circuit height is not what travels down the line.

(a) The first pulse covers $z_p = {{params.zp}}\ \mathrm{m}$ in ${{params.t1}}\ \mu\mathrm{s}$: $v = {{params.s_v}}\ \mathrm{m}/\mu\mathrm{s}$. Check with the second: $(2\ell - z_p)/v = {{params.s_back}}/{{params.s_v}} = {{params.t2}}\ \mu\mathrm{s}$. For a non-magnetic line $\epsilon_r = (c/v)^2 = {{params.s_epsr}}$.

(b) The second pulse is the first one after one reflection at the load: $\Gamma_L = {{{params.s_GL}}}$, so $R_L = Z_0\dfrac{1+\Gamma_L}{1-\Gamma_L} = {{params.s_RL}}\ \Omega$.

(c) No third pulse means the echo is not re-launched at the generator: $\Gamma_g = 0$, so $R_g = Z_0 = {{params.Z0}}\ \Omega$. Then the launched pulse is $A = {{params.V0}}\times\dfrac{Z_0}{R_g+Z_0} = {{params.s_A}}\ \mathrm{V}$: half of the source pulse stays across $R_g$.

(d) At the load the arriving pulse and its echo are present together: $A(1+\Gamma_L) = {{params.s_Vload}}\ \mathrm{V}$.

(e) With $R_g' = {{params.Rg2}}\ \Omega$ the launched pulse is ${{params.V0}}\times\dfrac{Z_0}{R_g'+Z_0} = {{params.s_A2}}\ \mathrm{V}$ and $\Gamma_g' = {{{params.s_Gg2}}}$. The third pulse has been reflected at the load and at the generator: ${{params.s_A2}}\times\Gamma_L\Gamma_g' = {{params.s_C}}\ \mathrm{V}$, after travelling $2\ell + z_p = {{params.s_third}}\ \mathrm{m}$: $t = {{params.s_t3}}\ \mu\mathrm{s}$.
