A uniform plane wave in {{{params.medium}}} has the electric field $$ {{{params.field_tex}}}\ \ \mathrm{V/m}, $$ with $\omega \gt 0$ and $\beta \gt 0$. Phasors follow $\mathbf{E} = \mathrm{Re}\{\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\,e^{j\omega t}\}$. Use $\eta_0 = 120\pi\ \Omega$.

Handedness: point your thumb along the direction of travel. If the fingers of your right hand curl the way the field turns at a fixed point, the wave is right-handed; if those of your left hand do, it is left-handed.

(a) Which is the phasor $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}$ of this field?

(c) Find the ${{params.w}}$ component of the time-average Poynting vector (signed: positive means along $+\hat{ {{params.w}} }$).

(d) Keep the form $\mathbf{E} = \hat{ {{params.a}} }\,A\cos(\cdot) + \hat{ {{params.b}} }\,B\sin(\cdot + \phi)$ with the same arguments and the same $A \gt 0$, but choose $B$ and $\phi$ freely. Which choice makes the wave {{params.tname}}?

Write the sine as a cosine first: $\sin x = \cos(x - 90^\circ)$. The phasor of $\cos(\omega t + \psi)$ is $e^{j\psi}$, and $e^{-j90^\circ} = -j$. The two perpendicular components do not interfere in the time average: add $A^2/2\eta$ and $B^2/2\eta$. The phase $\phi$ does not matter. Sit at one point and evaluate the field at $\omega t = 0$ and a quarter period later. Circular needs equal amplitudes and a quarter-cycle phase difference between two cosines. A magnetic field turns the same way as its electric field.

(a) $\sin(\theta+\phi) = \cos(\theta+\phi-90^\circ)$, whose phasor factor is $e^{j\phi}e^{-j90^\circ} = -j\,e^{j\phi}$; the travel along ${{{params.s_udir}}}$ sets the sign of the space factor: $\tilde{\mathbf{E}} = {{{params.s_correct_ph}}}$.

(b) At the origin at $t = 0$: $E_{ {{params.a}} } = {{{params.s_kA}}} = {{params.s_Ea0}}\ \mathrm{V/m}$ and $E_{ {{params.b}} } = {{{params.s_kB}}}\sin\phi = {{params.s_Eb0}}\ \mathrm{V/m}$. With $\mathbf{H} = \hat u\times\mathbf{E}/\eta$, $\hat u = {{{params.s_udir}}}$, $\eta = {{{params.s_etatex}}} = {{params.s_eta}}\ \Omega$, and ${{{params.s_x1}}}$, ${{{params.s_x2}}}$: $H_{ {{params.b}} } = {{params.s_Hb}}\ \mathrm{A/m}$ (from $E_{ {{params.a}} }$) and $H_{ {{params.a}} } = {{params.s_Ha}}\ \mathrm{A/m}$ (from $E_{ {{params.b}} }$).

(c) $\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle = \hat u\,\dfrac{A^2+B^2}{2\eta} = {{params.s_Smag}}\ \mathrm{W/m^2}$ along ${{{params.s_udir}}}$, so $\langle S_{ {{params.w}} }\rangle = {{params.s_Sw}}\ \mathrm{W/m^2}$.

(d) Circular needs $B = A$ and the second component a quarter cycle away from the first. Since the second component is already a sine, that means $\phi = 0^\circ$ or $180^\circ$; $\phi = \pm90^\circ$ turns the sine into $\pm$ a cosine and gives a linear wave. With ${{{params.s_circ}}}$ the field at a fixed point turns ${{{params.s_turn}}}$ in a quarter period, which is {{params.tname}} for travel along ${{{params.s_udir}}}$.

(e) $\phi = 0^\circ$ or $180^\circ$: linear, whatever $A$ and $B$ are. $A = B$ and $\phi = \pm90^\circ$: circular; the component that leads turns toward the one that lags, and the hand follows from the travel direction ${{{params.s3dir}}}$. Anything else: elliptical. Here: {{{params.s_rows}}}.
