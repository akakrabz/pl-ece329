A source with internal resistance $R_g = {{params.Z0}}\ \Omega$ drives a lossless line at $z = 0$. The line has $Z_0 = {{params.Z0}}\ \Omega$, wave speed $v = {{params.v}}\ \mathrm{m}/\mu\mathrm{s}$ and length $\ell = {{params.ell}}\ \mathrm{m}$, and its far end ($z = \ell$) is {{params.load_txt}}. The line is empty before $t = 0$.

$V(z,t)$ is the voltage of the upper conductor relative to the lower one, and $I(z,t)$ is the current along $+z$ (from the generator toward the far end) in the upper conductor. Times are in $\mu\mathrm{s}$.

(a) First let the source be a step, $V_g(t) = {{params.V0}}\,u(t)\ \mathrm{V}$. Find the steady-state ($t\to\infty$) voltage at the source end and current at the far end.

(b) Find the injection coefficient and the voltage reflection coefficients at the far end and at the generator.

(c) Now the source is a rectangular pulse: $V_g = {{params.V0}}\ \mathrm{V}$ for $0 \lt t \lt {{params.tp}}\ \mu\mathrm{s}$ and zero otherwise. Find the voltage and the current at the source end, $z = 0$, at $t_a = {{params.ta}}\ \mu\mathrm{s}$, $t_b = {{params.tb}}\ \mu\mathrm{s}$ and $t_c = {{params.tc}}\ \mu\mathrm{s}$.



(d) Which plot shows $V(0,t)$ for the pulse of part (c)? Here $V_0 = {{params.V0}}\ \mathrm{V}$ is the height of the source pulse.

A long time after a step, nothing changes any more: the line is just a pair of wires joining the generator to whatever sits at the far end. Solve that DC circuit. The generator is matched, so the echo is absorbed when it returns: there is the incident wave and one echo, which reaches $z = 0$ a time $2T$ after the part of the incident wave that produced it. A pulse is a step minus the same step delayed by the pulse length. For the current, the echo contributes $-V^-/Z_0$: at a short the currents of the incident wave and the echo add where their voltages cancel.

(a) DC circuit: {{{params.s_dc_why}}}. $V(0,\infty) = {{params.s_dcV}}\ \mathrm{V}$, $I(\ell,\infty) = {{params.s_dcI}}\ \mathrm{A}$.

(b) $\tau_g = Z_0/(R_g+Z_0) = \tfrac12$, $\Gamma_L = {{params.s_G}}$, $\Gamma_g = 0$ (matched). The one-way delay is $T = \ell/v = {{params.s_T}}\ \mu\mathrm{s}$, so the echo of anything launched returns after $2T = {{params.s_2T}}\ \mu\mathrm{s}$.

(c) The incident wave has $V^+ = {{params.s_h}}\ \mathrm{V}$ and $I^+ = V^+/Z_0 = {{params.s_Ih}}\ \mathrm{A}$ and is at $z = 0$ for $0 \lt t \lt {{params.tp}}\ \mu\mathrm{s}$. The echo has $V^- = \Gamma_L V^+ = {{params.s_echoV}}\ \mathrm{V}$ and $I^- = -V^-/Z_0 = {{params.s_echoI}}\ \mathrm{A}$ and is at $z = 0$ for ${{params.s_2T}} \lt t \lt {{params.s_end}}\ \mu\mathrm{s}$.

(d) The record is the incident pulse plus its echo delayed by $2T$, with the two added where they overlap. It returns to zero only at ${{params.s_end}}\ \mu\mathrm{s}$, a time $2T$ after the source switches off.

(e) The far end takes no power ($V = 0$ at a short, $I = 0$ at an open end), the line is lossless and ends up empty, so all the energy supplied by the source ends as heat in $R_g$: part while the pulse is launched, the rest when the echo is absorbed.
