Three lossless line sections are joined in cascade between a generator and a resistive load, as shown; $T_1$, $T_2$, $T_3$ are their one-way delays. At $t = 0$ the generator produces one very narrow rectangular pulse of open-circuit height ${{params.V0}}\ \mathrm{V}$ (much shorter than any of the delays). The lines are empty before $t = 0$.

{{{params.svg}}}

Reflection and transmission coefficients are voltage ratios unless a part says otherwise. The subscript $jk$ refers to a wave travelling in section $j$ and arriving at the junction with section $k$. Currents are positive along $+z$ (toward the load) in the upper conductor.

(a) Find the injection coefficient $\tau_g$ and the height of the pulse launched onto section 1.

(b) Find the voltage reflection and transmission coefficients at junction 1 for a wave arriving from section 1, and the current reflection coefficient at junction 2 for a wave arriving from section 2.

(c) Find the earliest time at which an echo produced at junction 2 arrives back at the generator end, and the voltage of that echo as a wave on section 1 (just before it reaches the generator).

(d) Find the time at which the pulse first reaches the load, and the height of the voltage pulse across the load at that moment.

(e) The first echo of all returns to the generator at $t = {{params.t2T1}}\ \mu\mathrm{s}$. Find the height of the voltage pulse seen at the generator end ($z = 0$) at that moment.

A wave that reaches a junction sees the next section as a resistor equal to that section's characteristic impedance, whatever is connected further on. Use $\Gamma = (Z_{\text{ahead}} - Z_{\text{here}})/(Z_{\text{ahead}} + Z_{\text{here}})$ and $\tau = 1 + \Gamma$. The height of a pulse that has followed a given path is the launched height times one coefficient per junction or end it met: a $\tau$ each time it crossed, a $\Gamma$ each time it turned back. On the way back through junction 1 the pulse comes from section 2, so the coefficient is $\tau_{21}$, not $\tau_{12}$. A backward wave carries $I = -V/Z$, so the current reflection coefficient is $-\Gamma$. For transmission, divide each voltage by its own section's impedance.

(a) At $t = 0$ the generator sees only $Z_1$: $\tau_g = \dfrac{Z_1}{R_g+Z_1} = {{{params.s_tg}}}$ and $V_1^+ = \tau_g\times{{params.V0}} = {{params.s_V1}}\ \mathrm{V}$.

(b) $\Gamma_{12} = \dfrac{Z_2-Z_1}{Z_2+Z_1} = {{{params.s_G12}}}$, $\tau_{12} = 1+\Gamma_{12} = {{{params.s_t12}}}$. At junction 2, $\Gamma_{23} = \dfrac{Z_3-Z_2}{Z_3+Z_2} = {{{params.s_G23}}}$ for voltage, so for current $\Gamma^I_{23} = -\Gamma_{23} = {{{params.s_GI23}}}$.

(c) Out through sections 1 and 2 and back: $t = 2(T_1+T_2) = {{params.s_techo}}\ \mu\mathrm{s}$. The pulse crossed junction 1 ($\tau_{12}$), turned back at junction 2 ($\Gamma_{23}$) and crossed junction 1 again from the other side ($\tau_{21} = 1-\Gamma_{12} = {{{params.s_t21}}}$): $V_1^+\,\tau_{12}\Gamma_{23}\tau_{21} = {{params.s_echo}}\ \mathrm{V}$.

(d) $t = T_1+T_2+T_3 = {{params.s_tload}}\ \mu\mathrm{s}$. The pulse arriving on section 3 is $V_1^+\tau_{12}\tau_{23}$ with $\tau_{23} = {{{params.s_t23}}}$, and at the load it adds to its own reflection ($\Gamma_L = {{{params.s_GL}}}$): $V_1^+\tau_{12}\tau_{23}(1+\Gamma_L) = {{params.s_Vload}}\ \mathrm{V}$.

(e) The first echo comes from junction 1: $\Gamma_{12}V_1^+ = {{params.s_first}}\ \mathrm{V}$, arriving at $2T_1$. {{{params.s_matched_g}}} With $\Gamma_g = {{{params.s_Gg}}}$ the voltage at $z = 0$ is $\Gamma_{12}V_1^+(1+\Gamma_g) = {{params.s_Vgen}}\ \mathrm{V}$.

(f) $\dfrac{I_t}{I_i} = \dfrac{V_t/Z_2}{V_i/Z_1} = \tau_{12}\dfrac{Z_1}{Z_2} = \dfrac{2Z_1}{Z_1+Z_2} = 1-\Gamma_{12} = {{params.s_tau_i}}$. The voltage and current transmission coefficients differ whenever the two impedances differ.
