Compare with $e^{-\alpha z}e^{-j\beta z}$. The first exponential is the envelope, the second the phase. $\alpha = \beta = \sqrt{\pi f\mu\sigma} = 1/\delta$ and $\eta = (1+j)\,\alpha/\sigma$. The second one follows from $\gamma\eta = j\omega\mu$ or from $\gamma/\eta \approx \sigma$. $\langle\mathbf{S}\rangle = \tfrac12\mathrm{Re}\{\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\times\tilde{\mathbf{H}}^*\}$ contains $\lvert\tilde E\rvert^2$, so it decays as $e^{-2\alpha z}$, and it carries a factor $\cos\angle\eta$. In decibels, $10\log_{10}e^{2\alpha z} = 8.686\,\alpha z$.

(a) $\alpha = \beta$ is the good-conductor limit: $\gamma = \sqrt{j\omega\mu\sigma} = (1+j)\sqrt{\pi f\mu\sigma}$ when $\sigma \gg \omega\epsilon$. Then $\eta = j\omega\mu/\gamma$ has the angle $90^\circ - 45^\circ = 45^\circ$.

(b) $\alpha = \beta = {{params.s_a}}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}$, so $\delta = 1/\alpha = {{params.s_delta}}\ \mathrm{m}$ and $\lambda = 2\pi/\beta = 2\pi\delta = {{params.s_lam}}\ \mathrm{m}$. From $\alpha^2 = \pi f\mu_0\sigma$: $f = \alpha^2/(\pi\mu_0\sigma) = {{params.s_f}}\ \mathrm{kHz}$. From $\eta = (1+j)\alpha/\sigma$: $\lvert\eta\rvert = \sqrt2\,\alpha/\sigma = {{{params.s_eta}}}\ \Omega$.

(c) $\tilde{\mathbf{H}} = \hat z\times\tilde{\mathbf{E}}/\eta$, so $\langle S_z\rangle = \dfrac{\lvert\tilde E\rvert^2}{2\lvert\eta\rvert}\cos45^\circ = \dfrac{\sigma E_0^2}{4\alpha}\,e^{-2\alpha z}$. At $z = 0$: ${{params.s_S0}}\ {{{params.S_unit}}}$. At $z = {{params.d_s}}\ \mathrm{m}$, $2\alpha z = {{params.s_md}}$: ${{params.s_S0}}\,e^{-{{params.s_md}}} = {{params.s_Sd}}\ {{{params.S_unit}}}$.

(d) The power falls as $e^{-2\alpha z}$: $10\log_{10}e^{2\alpha} = 8.686\,\alpha = {{params.s_dBm}}\ \mathrm{dB/m}$. It is down {{params.XdB}} dB at $z = {{params.XdB}}/{{params.s_dBm}} = {{params.s_zX}}\ \mathrm{m}$. (The field, in contrast, falls as $e^{-\alpha z}$.)

(e) By Poynting's theorem the heat in the layer is what flows in at the top minus what flows out at the bottom: ${{params.s_S0}} - {{params.s_Sd}} = {{params.s_Qd}}\ {{{params.S_unit}}}$. The same number comes from $\int_0^d\tfrac12\sigma\lvert\tilde E\rvert^2\,dz$.

(f) $\delta = 1/\sqrt{\pi f\mu\sigma}$, and {{{params.s_why}}}: the skin depth becomes ${{{params.s_ratio}}}\,\delta$.
