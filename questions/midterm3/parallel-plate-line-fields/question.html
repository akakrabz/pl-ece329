A parallel-plate transmission line runs along $z$. Its perfectly conducting plates lie in the planes $x = 0$ (bottom) and $x = d$ (top), with $d = {{params.d_s}}\ \mathrm{mm}$; each is $W = {{params.W_s}}\ \mathrm{cm}$ wide along $y$. The gap is filled with {{params.fill}} ($\epsilon_r = {{params.epsr_s}}$, $\mu = \mu_0$). Because $W \gg d$, the fields between the plates are uniform, $\mathbf{E} = \hat x\,E_x$ and $\mathbf{H} = \hat y\,H_y$, and are zero outside.

{{{params.svg}}}

$V$ is the voltage of the top plate relative to the bottom plate, and $I$ is the current flowing along $+z$ in the top plate. Use $c = 3\times10^{8}\ \mathrm{m/s}$, $\eta_0 = 120\pi\ \Omega$ and $\epsilon_0 = 10^{-9}/36\pi\ \mathrm{F/m}$.

(a) Find the characteristic impedance of the line and the speed of waves on it.

(b) A single pulse travels toward ${{params.zdir}}$. At one cross-section, at one instant, the {{params.plate}} plate carries a total current of ${{params.I1_s}}\ \mathrm{A}$ flowing in the ${{params.idir}}$ direction (spread evenly over its inner face). Find, at that cross-section and instant, the fields between the plates (signed), the voltage $V$, and the surface charge density on the inner face of the top plate.

(d) The pulse is replaced by a sinusoidal wave with $\tilde{\mathbf{E}} = \hat x\,{{{params.E0_tex}}}\,e^{-j\beta z}\ \mathrm{V/m}$ between the plates. Find the amplitude of the voltage between the plates, and the time-average power the wave carries through a cross-section of the line.

$V = E\,d$ and $I = H\,W$, and the fields of a TEM wave obey $E/H = \eta$. So $Z_0 = \eta\,d/W$: separation over width, not the other way round. On a perfect conductor $\mathbf{J}_s = \hat n\times\mathbf{H}$, with $\hat n$ pointing out of the metal into the field region: $-\hat x$ on the top plate, $+\hat x$ on the bottom plate. The total current is $J_s W$. $\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{H}$ points along the travel, which fixes the sign of $E_x$. Then $V = -\int_{\text{bottom}}^{\text{top}}\mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = -E_x d$ and $\rho_s = \hat n\cdot\mathbf{D}$ on the top plate.

(a) $\eta = {{{params.s_eta_tex}}} = {{params.s_eta}}\ \Omega$, so $Z_0 = \eta\,d/W = {{params.s_eta}}\times{{{params.s_dW}}} = {{params.s_Z0}}\ \Omega$, and $v = c/\sqrt{\epsilon_r} = {{params.s_v}}\times10^{8}\ \mathrm{m/s}$.

(b) The given current is ${{params.s_Igiven}}\ \mathrm{A}$ along $+z$ in the {{params.plate}} plate. $\mathbf{J}_s = \hat n\times\mathbf{H}$ on the {{{params.s_plate_rule}}}: $H_y = {{params.s_Hy}}\ \mathrm{A/m}$. (The other plate carries the opposite current, so $I = {{params.s_Itop}}\ \mathrm{A}$.) The wave travels toward ${{params.zdir}}$, so $\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{H} = E_xH_y\,\hat z$ must point that way: $E_x = {{params.s_s}}\eta H_y = {{params.s_Ex}}\ \mathrm{V/m}$. Then $V = -E_x d = {{params.s_V}}\ \mathrm{V}$, and on the top plate ($\hat n = -\hat x$) $\rho_s = -\epsilon E_x = {{params.s_rho}}\ \mathrm{nC/m^2}$.

(c) $V/I = {{params.s_V}}/({{params.s_Itop}}) = {{params.s_sZ0}}\ \Omega = {{params.s_s}}Z_0$: a wave toward $+z$ has $V/I = +Z_0$, a wave toward $-z$ has $V/I = -Z_0$.

(d) $\lvert\tilde V\rvert = E_0 d = {{params.s_V0}}\ \mathrm{V}$. The power density is $E_0^2/2\eta$ over the area $W d$: $\langle P\rangle = \dfrac{E_0^2}{2\eta}\,W d = {{params.s_P}}\ \mathrm{W}$, the same as $\lvert\tilde V\rvert^2/2Z_0$.
