An infinitely thin sheet in the plane ${{params.n}} = 0$ carries the surface current $$\mathbf{J}_s(t) = {{params.J_sign}}J_0\,g(t)\,{{params.a_vec}},\qquad J_0 = {{params.J0}}\ \mathrm{mA/m}.$$ The waveform $g(t)$ (figure) is zero for $t \lt 0$. It rises linearly to $1$ at $t = {{params.tr}}\ \mathrm{ns}$, {{{params.flat_desc}}}falls linearly back to $0$ at $t = {{params.T}}\ \mathrm{ns}$, and stays zero afterwards. All space on both sides of the sheet is filled with {{{params.medium}}}; the medium is lossless and non-magnetic ($\mu = \mu_0$). Use $c = 3.00\times10^{8}\ \mathrm{m/s}$ and $\eta_0 = 120\pi\ \Omega$. Positions are in metres and times in nanoseconds.

Each face of the sheet launches a wave travelling away from it. With $\hat{n}$ the unit normal pointing from the sheet toward the field point and $d$ the distance from the sheet, $\mathbf{E} = -\tfrac{\eta}{2}\mathbf{J}_s(t - d/v)$ and $\mathbf{H} = \tfrac12\mathbf{J}_s(t - d/v)\times\hat{n}$, where $v = c/\sqrt{\epsilon_r}$ and $\eta = \eta_0/\sqrt{\epsilon_r}$. What a point at distance $d$ sees at time $t$ left the sheet at $t - d/v$. In a snapshot at time $t_1$, a feature emitted at time $t'$ sits at distance $v(t_1 - t')$ from the sheet, so the start of the pulse has travelled farthest.

Medium: $v = c/\sqrt{\epsilon_r} = {{params.v}}\ \mathrm{m/ns}$ and $\eta = \eta_0/\sqrt{\epsilon_r} = {{params.eta_tex}} = {{params.eta}}\ \Omega$, so $\eta/2 = {{params.eta_half}}\ \Omega$.

(a) While $g \gt 0$ the current flows along ${{params.J_dir}}$. $\mathbf{E} = -\tfrac{\eta}{2}\mathbf{J}_s$ points opposite to it, along ${{params.Ea_dir}}$, on both sides: tangential $\mathbf{E}$ is continuous. For $\mathbf{H} = \tfrac12\mathbf{J}_s\times\hat{n}$, use $\hat{n} = {{params.np_vec}}$ on the side ${{params.n}} \gt 0$, which gives ${{params.Hp_dir}}$, and $\hat{n} = {{params.nm_vec}}$ on the side ${{params.n}} \lt 0$, which gives ${{params.Hm_dir}}$. $\mathbf{H}$ reverses, and its tangential jump equals $J_s$. Check: $\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{H}$ points along ${{params.S_plus}}$ for ${{params.n}} \gt 0$ and along ${{params.S_minus}}$ for ${{params.n}} \lt 0$, away from the sheet on both sides.

(d) A point at distance $d = {{params.dq}}\ \mathrm{m}$ sees the source waveform delayed by $d/v = {{params.dq}}/{{params.v}} = {{params.t_on}}\ \mathrm{ns}$, on either side of the sheet. So the fields are nonzero for ${{params.t_on}}\ \mathrm{ns} \lt t \lt {{params.t_on}} + {{params.T}} = {{params.t_off}}\ \mathrm{ns}$. A record keeps the waveform's time order: it rises first.

(e) At $t_1 = {{params.t1}}$ ns, the start of the pulse, emitted at $t' = 0$, has reached $v t_1 = {{params.front}}$ m from the sheet. Its end, emitted at $t' = {{params.T}}$ ns, is at $v(t_1 - {{params.T}}) = {{params.back}}$ m. The same happens on both sides. A feature emitted at $t'$ sits at $v(t_1 - t')$, so the snapshot is the waveform mirrored, with the leading edge outermost. The correct description is: {{{params.sketch_ok}}}. The fields do not vanish when the current stops; they keep travelling outward.
