(e) Now both coils are driven by current sources, with $dI_1/dt = {{params.al1}}\ \mathrm{A/s}$ and $dI_2/dt = {{params.al2}}\ \mathrm{A/s}$ ($I_2$ still counted in $I_1$'s sense). A single circular wire loop of radius $r_0 = {{params.r0}}\ \mathrm{cm}$, coaxial with the solenoids (dashed in the figure), is traversed in the same sense as $I_1$. Find the emf around the loop in that direction.

An ideal solenoid has $\mathbf{H} = nI$ along its axis inside its own winding (direction by the right-hand rule: fingers along the current, thumb along $\mathbf{H}$) and $\mathbf{H} = 0$ outside it. At a given radius, add only the coils whose winding encloses that radius. The flux through a coaxial loop is $\int\mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{S}$ over the disk it bounds: each coil contributes $\mu_0 nI$ times the part of the disk that lies inside that coil's winding. A loop that encloses a coil links all of that coil's flux, whether or not the field reaches the wire. Then $\mathcal{E} = -d\Psi/dt$, with $d\mathbf{S}$ along the direction the right-hand rule assigns to the loop's sense of traversal.

Directions. $I_1$ circulates {{params.sense1}} seen from $+{{params.axis}}$, so by the right-hand rule a positive current (in $I_1$'s sense) makes $\mathbf{H}$ along ${{{params.s1_tex}}}$ inside its winding. Each ideal coil contributes $nI$ inside its winding and nothing outside.

(a) For $r \lt a_2$ both coils contribute: $H_{ {{params.axis}} } = {{params.s1_sign}}(n_1I_1 + n_2I_2) = {{params.s1_sign}}\big({{params.n1I1}} + ({{params.n2I2}})\big)\ \mathrm{A/m} = {{params.Ha_str}}\ \mathrm{kA/m}$.

(b) $n_1I_1 + n_2I_2 = 0$ gives $I_2 = -n_1I_1/n_2 = -({{params.n1}})({{params.I1}})/{{params.n2}} = {{params.I2c_str}}\ \mathrm{A}$, i.e. {{{params.I2c_words}}}: the inner coil's field must point against the outer coil's.

(c) For $a_2 \lt r \lt a_1$ only the outer coil's winding encloses the point; the inner coil's field stops at its own winding, whatever $I_2$ is. So $H_{ {{params.axis}} } = {{params.s1_sign}}n_1I_1 = {{params.Hc_str}}\ \mathrm{kA/m}$ (and $\mathbf{H} = 0$ for $r \gt a_1$).

(d) Each turn of the inner coil links the outer coil's flux $\mu_0n_1I_1\pi a_2^2$, which is {{params.lenz_words}}. By Faraday's law each turn has an emf $-d\Psi/dt$, and by Lenz's rule the induced current produces a field that opposes the change in flux: it circulates {{{params.lenz_resp}}}. The outer coil's field is uniform over the inner coil's cross-section, but it changes in time, which is all Faraday's law needs.
