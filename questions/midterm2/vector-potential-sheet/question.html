(e) Now let the strength of the potential ramp steadily in time, $\mathbf{A}(t) = {{{params.A_t_tex}}}$ mWb/m ($t$ in seconds), that is $dk/dt = {{params.beta_str}}\ {{{params.beta_unit}}}$, with scalar potential $\Phi = 0$. Find the component of $\mathbf{E}$ along ${{{params.ahat}}}$ at $P_1$.

$\mathbf{A}$ has a single component, $A_{ {{params.a}} }$, which depends only on ${{params.u}}$. In the divergence that component is differentiated with respect to ${{params.a}}$; in the curl it is differentiated with respect to ${{params.u}}$. Write out the component of $\nabla\times\mathbf{A}$ along the third axis, ${{params.cax}}$, keeping its sign. A jump in tangential $\mathbf{H}$ is a surface current, $\mathbf{J}_s = \hat{n}\times(\mathbf{H}_1 - \mathbf{H}_2)$ with $\hat{n}$ pointing from region 2 into region 1; a smooth $\mathbf{H}$ gives $\mathbf{J} = \nabla\times\mathbf{H}$. Either way, $\nabla^2\mathbf{A} = -\mu_0\mathbf{J}$ is a cross-check (mind the minus sign). With time variation, $\mathbf{E} = -\nabla\Phi - \partial\mathbf{A}/\partial t$.

(a) $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{A} = \partial A_{ {{params.a}} }/\partial {{params.a}} = 0$: the only component, $A_{ {{params.a}} } = {{params.k_str}}\,{{{params.f_tex}}}$, does not depend on ${{params.a}}$. So this $\mathbf{A}$ satisfies the Coulomb gauge everywhere. (The derivative with respect to ${{params.u}}$ belongs in the curl; the second derivative belongs in $\nabla^2\mathbf{A}$.)
