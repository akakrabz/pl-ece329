When $\mathbf{B}$ varies linearly with position, its average over any flat loop equals its value at the loop's centre, so $\Psi = A\,\hat{n}\cdot\mathbf{B}(\text{centre})$ exactly. A current needs this to change in time: ask how $\hat{n}\cdot\mathbf{B}$ at the centre changes as the centre moves. $\mathcal{E} = -d\Psi/dt$, with $\Psi$ computed using the $d\mathbf{S}$ that the right-hand rule ties to $C$. Check the direction with Lenz's rule (the induced current's own field opposes the change in flux), or with $\oint(\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B})\cdot d\mathbf{l}$, which here picks up only the two edges perpendicular to $\mathbf{v}$.
