A wave arriving on line $j$ sees, until an echo returns, the impedance $Z_{eq}$ of everything beyond the junction: line $k$ as a resistor $Z_k$, in parallel with any resistor across the junction. Then $\Gamma_{jk} = (Z_{eq}-Z_j)/(Z_{eq}+Z_j)$, and since the voltage is the same on both sides of the junction, $\tau_{jk} = 1 + \Gamma_{jk}$. One-way times $T_1 = \ell_1/v_1$ and $T_2 = \ell_2/v_2$. The generator launches $\tau_gV_0$ with $\tau_g = Z_1/(R_g+Z_1)$ and reflects with $\Gamma_g$ computed with $Z_1$; the load reflects with $\Gamma_L$ computed with $Z_2$. Each wave arriving at the junction raises the junction voltage by $\tau$ times its own voltage. At DC both lines are wires.
