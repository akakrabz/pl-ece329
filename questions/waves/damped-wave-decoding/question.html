$\omega$ multiplies $t$; $\beta$ multiplies the position in the phase; $\alpha$ multiplies it in the decaying exponential. Then $\eta = j\omega\mu/\gamma$: $\lvert\eta\rvert = \omega\mu/\lvert\gamma\rvert$ and $\tau = 90^\circ - \angle\gamma = \tan^{-1}(\alpha/\beta)$. Use this medium's $\mu$, ${{{params.mu_tex}}}$. $\tilde{\mathbf{H}} = \hat{u}\times\tilde{\mathbf{E}}/\eta$ (divide by $\lvert\eta\rvert$, subtract $\tau$) or $\tilde{\mathbf{E}} = \eta\,\tilde{\mathbf{H}}\times\hat{u}$ (multiply by $\lvert\eta\rvert$, add $\tau$), with $\hat{u}$ the direction of travel; both decay as $e^{-\alpha d}$. For the medium, $\gamma/\eta = \gamma^2/(j\omega\mu) = \sigma + j\omega\epsilon$, so $\sigma = 2\alpha\beta/\omega\mu$ and $\epsilon = (\beta^2-\alpha^2)/\omega^2\mu$.

(a) {{{params.read_txt}}} The field direction ${{{params.F_tex}}}$ is the polarization, not the travel, and decay does not make a standing wave: the phase still moves.

(b) $\alpha = {{params.alpha_str}}\ \mathrm{Np/m}$ (in the exponential) and $\beta = {{{params.beta_eq}}}\ \mathrm{rad/m}$ (in the phase): {{{params.tri_txt}}}. Then $\lambda = 2\pi/\beta = {{params.lam_str}}\ \mathrm{m}$ and $\delta = 1/\alpha = {{params.delta_str}}\ \mathrm{m}$.
