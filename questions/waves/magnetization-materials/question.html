{{#params.facts}}{{{text}}} {{/params.facts}}
The magnetization current is $\nabla\times\mathbf{M}$ in the volume and $\mathbf{J}_{sM} = \mathbf{M}\times\hat{n}$ on a surface, with $\hat{n}$ the outward normal. A uniformly magnetized rod is therefore a solenoid with $nI$ replaced by $M$. Then use $\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{B}/\mu_0-\mathbf{M}$. $B_n$ is continuous ($\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0$). With no free surface current tangential $\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{B}/\mu$ is continuous, so tangential $\mathbf{B}$ is multiplied by $\mu_2/\mu_1$. In a linear medium $\mathbf{M} = \chi_m\mathbf{H}$ with $\chi_m = \mu_r-1$, and no $\mu_0$.

(a) Inside the rod $\mathbf{M}$ is uniform, so $\nabla\times\mathbf{M} = \mathbf{0}$: there is no volume current. On the end faces the outward normal is $\pm\hat{ {{params.u}} }$, parallel to $\mathbf{M}$, so $\mathbf{M}\times\hat{n} = \mathbf{0}$. On the curved wall $\hat{n} = \hat{r}$, and since $\hat{ {{params.u}} }\times\hat{r} = \hat{\phi}$, $$\mathbf{J}_{sM} = \mathbf{M}\times\hat{r} = {{{params.sgn}}}{{params.M0}}\,\hat{ {{params.u}} }\times\hat{r} = {{{params.sgn}}}{{params.M0}}\,\hat{\phi}\ \ \mathrm{kA/m},$$ of magnitude $M_0 = {{params.M0}}\ \mathrm{kA/m}$. The current circulates around the side wall exactly like the winding of a solenoid, with $nI$ replaced by $M_0$. An air-core solenoid with the same $\mathbf{B}$ needs $nI = M_0$, so $I = M_0/n = ({{params.M0}}\times10^{3}\ \mathrm{A/m})/({{params.n_turns}}\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}) = {{params.Ieq_str}}\ \mathrm{A}$. This shows how strong a permanent magnet is. The tempting wrong answers: $-\hat{\phi}$ comes from writing $\hat{n}\times\mathbf{M}$; "nowhere" forgets that $\mathbf{M}$ ends at the wall; the end faces carry nothing because $\mathbf{M}\parallel\hat{n}$ there.

(b) Far from the ends the equivalent solenoid looks infinite: $\mathbf{B}\approx\mu_0\mathbf{M}$ inside and $\mathbf{B}\approx\mathbf{0}$ just outside the side wall. So $B_{ {{params.u}} } = \mu_0 M_{ {{params.u}} } = (4\pi\times10^{-7})({{{params.sgn}}}{{params.M0}}\times10^{3})\ \mathrm{T} = {{params.Bin_str}}\ \mathrm{mT}$ (the rod is $\ell/a\ge 40$ radii long, so the end correction is below 0.2 %). Then $\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{B}/\mu_0-\mathbf{M}\approx\mathbf{M}-\mathbf{M} = \mathbf{0}$: a permanent magnet sitting at its remanence. "$\mathbf{H}\approx\mathbf{M}$" uses the vacuum relation inside matter. "$\tfrac12\mu_0\mathbf{M}$, $-\tfrac12\mathbf{M}$" are the values at the centre of an end face. "$\mathbf{B}\approx\mathbf{0}$, $\mathbf{H}\approx-\mathbf{M}$" describes a thin plate magnetized through its thickness. And a closed cylindrical current sheet confines its field inside, unlike a flat sheet.

(c) The sign of $\chi_m = \mu_r-1$ decides between dia- and paramagnetic; $\chi_m\gg1$ means ferromagnetic.
