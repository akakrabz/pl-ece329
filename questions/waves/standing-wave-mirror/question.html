A perfect conductor reflects each tangential component with $\Gamma = -1$, and the reflected $\tilde{\mathbf{H}}$ keeps the incident sign. With $D$ the distance from the mirror, the total $\lvert\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\rvert\propto\lvert\sin\beta D\rvert$ and $\lvert\tilde{\mathbf{H}}\rvert\propto\lvert\cos\beta D\rvert$, each with twice the incident amplitude. $\mathbf{J}_s = \hat n\times\mathbf{H}$ on the surface, with $\hat n$ pointing out of the conductor into the air. The average pressure on a perfect mirror is $2\langle S_i\rangle/c$, with $\langle S_i\rangle$ the incident wave's average flux (a circular wave carries both components' power).

The incident wave travels along $\hat u_i = {{{params.ui}}}$, toward the mirror. In air $\lambda = c/f = {{params.lam_str}}\ \mathrm{cm}$ and $\beta = 2\pi/\lambda = {{params.beta_str}}\ \mathrm{rad/m}$. The reflected wave ($\Gamma = -1$ for each tangential component, travel along $\hat u_r = {{{params.ur}}}$) is $$\mathbf{E}_r = {{{params.Er_tex}}},$$ and the totals in the air, from $\tilde{\mathbf{E}}_i + \tilde{\mathbf{E}}_r$ and $\tilde{\mathbf{H}} = \hat u\times\tilde{\mathbf{E}}/\eta_0$ for each wave, are $$\mathbf{E} = {{{params.Etot_tex}}},$$ $$\mathbf{H} = {{{params.Htot_tex}}}.$$ Each is a function of position times a function of time: a standing wave.

(a) $\lvert\tilde{\mathbf{E}}\rvert\propto\lvert\sin\beta D\rvert$ vanishes at $D = 0, \lambda/2, \lambda, \ldots$: the first nonzero null is at $\lambda/2 = {{params.dE_str}}\ \mathrm{cm}$. $\lvert\tilde{\mathbf{H}}\rvert\propto\lvert\cos\beta D\rvert$ vanishes first at $\lambda/4 = {{params.dH_str}}\ \mathrm{cm}$; the mirror itself is an $\mathbf{H}$ maximum.

(b) $D = {{params.dp_str}}\ \mathrm{cm} = ({{params.r_tex}})\lambda$, so $\beta D = {{params.phase_str}}^\circ$, $\lvert\sin\beta D\rvert = {{params.sin_str}}$ and $\lvert\cos\beta D\rvert = {{params.cos_str}}$. Then $\lvert\mathbf{E}\rvert_{\max} = 2E_0\lvert\sin\beta D\rvert = {{params.Emax_str}}\ \mathrm{V/m}$ and $\lvert\mathbf{H}\rvert_{\max} = (2E_0/\eta_0)\lvert\cos\beta D\rvert = {{params.Hmax_str}}\ \mathrm{mA/m}$.{{#params.circ}} For this circular wave both magnitudes are in fact constant in time.{{/params.circ}}{{#params.lin}} They occur a quarter period apart.{{/params.lin}}

(c) {{#params.circ}}On the mirror, $\mathbf{E}_i$ points along ${{{params.e0_dir}}}$ at $t = 0$ and along ${{{params.eq_dir}}}$ at $t = T/4$; their cross product points along ${{{params.cross_dir}}}$, and its dot product with $\hat u_i = {{{params.ui}}}$ is {{params.dot_str}}: the incident wave is {{params.hand_i}}-circular. On the mirror $\mathbf{E}_r = -\mathbf{E}_i$, which rotates the same way, but the reflected wave travels along ${{{params.ur}}}$, so the dot product changes sign: the reflected wave is {{params.hand_r}}-circular.{{/params.circ}} {{#params.lin}}$\Gamma = -1$ is real, so the reflected wave is linearly polarized along the same axis $\hat{ {{params.a}} }$, and on the mirror $\mathbf{E}_r = -\mathbf{E}_i$ at every instant, which keeps the tangential $\mathbf{E}$ zero. Its $\mathbf{H}$, along $\hat{ {{params.b}} }$, keeps the incident sign.{{/params.lin}}

(d) On the mirror $\mathbf{H}(0,t)$ is twice the incident $\mathbf{H}$. With $\hat n = {{{params.nhat}}}$ (out of the conductor), $$\mathbf{J}_s = \hat n\times\mathbf{H}(0,t) = \frac{2\mathbf{E}_i(0,t)}{\eta_0} = {{{params.Ji_tex}}}.$$ Its largest magnitude is $2E_0/\eta_0 = {{params.Js_str}}\ \mathrm{mA/m}$, twice the incident $\lvert\mathbf{H}\rvert_{\max} = {{params.Hi_str}}\ \mathrm{mA/m}$. At $t = {{params.t1_tex}}$ it points along ${{{params.J_dir}}}$, while $\mathbf{H}$ on the surface points along ${{{params.H_dir}}}$.

(e) $\langle S_i\rangle = {{{params.hfac}}}E_0^2/2\eta_0 = {{params.Si_str}}\ \mathrm{W/m^2}${{#params.circ}} (two equal components){{/params.circ}}, so $\langle P_{rad}\rangle = 2\langle S_i\rangle/c = {{params.P_str}}\ \mathrm{nPa}$, pushing along ${{{params.ui}}}$, into the conductor. On {{params.A_m2}} $\mathrm{m^2}$: $\lvert\langle\mathbf{F}\rangle\rvert = {{params.F_str}}\ \mathrm{nN}$. The same follows from the force on the sheet current, $P(t) = \tfrac12\mu_0\lvert\mathbf{H}(0,t)\rvert^2$, averaged over a period.
