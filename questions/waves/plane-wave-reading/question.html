{{#params.facts}}{{{text}}} {{/params.facts}}
A fixed value of $s$ (for instance the peak, $s = 0$) marks a plane that moves. Solve "numerator $= 0$" for the position as a function of $t$. Equivalently: opposite signs on the $t$ and position terms mean travel toward $+$ that axis, the same sign toward $-$, and the speed is the ratio of the two coefficients. An overall minus sign or the order of the terms changes nothing. For one travelling wave $\mathbf{H} = \dfrac{1}{\eta}\,\hat{u}\times\mathbf{E}$, with $\hat{u}$ the direction of travel and $\eta = \sqrt{\mu/\epsilon} = \eta_0/\sqrt{\epsilon_r} = \mu_0 v$ here. Then check that $\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{H}$ points along $\hat{u}$.

(a) {{{params.read_txt}}} Not "along $\mathbf{E}$": the polarization is not the travel direction; and the pulse cannot stand still, because $s$ mixes $t$ and position.

(b) $v = {{params.v_str}}\ \mathrm{m/ns} = {{params.v8_str}}\times10^{8}\ \mathrm{m/s}$. With $\mu_r = 1$, $v = c/\sqrt{\epsilon_r}$, so $\epsilon_r = (c/v)^2 = (2.998/{{params.v8_str}})^2 = {{params.eps_str}}$.

(c) $\eta = \eta_0/\sqrt{\epsilon_r} = \mu_0 v = {{params.eta_str}}\ \Omega$ (with $\eta_0 = {{params.eta0_str}}\ \Omega$). At the peak $\lvert\mathbf{E}\rvert = {{params.E0}}\ \mathrm{V/m}$, so $\lvert\mathbf{H}\rvert = {{params.E0}}/{{params.eta_str}} = {{params.H_str}}\ \mathrm{mA/m}$. Direction: $\mathbf{H} = \tfrac{1}{\eta}\,\hat{u}\times\mathbf{E}$ with $\hat{u} = {{{params.trav_hat}}}$ and $\mathbf{E}$ along ${{{params.E_hat}}}$ at the peak, so $\mathbf{H}$ points along $({{{params.trav_hat}}})\times({{{params.E_hat}}}) = {{{params.h_hat}}}$. Check: $({{{params.E_hat}}})\times({{{params.h_hat}}}) = {{{params.trav_hat}}}$, the direction of travel. $\mathbf{H}$ has no component along the travel or along $\mathbf{E}$, and in a single travelling wave it peaks together with $\mathbf{E}$.
